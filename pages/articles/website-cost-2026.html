How Much Does a Business Website Cost in 2026?

There is no single price for a business website. The right budget depends on what the site needs to do, how much custom design and development is involved, and what happens after launch.

Published by WebcraftUpdated 2026

If you are comparing website options for a business, it is easy to find wildly different prices. That is because a website is not a single product: a simple brochure site, a custom business platform and an online store can require very different amounts of design, development and ongoing work.

What determines the cost of a website?

The biggest factors are usually the scope of the project and the amount of custom work involved.

Design

Custom layouts, visual direction, responsive behavior and interaction design take more work than adapting a simple template.

Development

Pages, forms, dashboards, integrations, search, accounts and other functionality add development time and testing requirements.

Content

Copywriting, product information, photography, graphics and other content can be part of the project or supplied by the business.

Integrations

Payments, forms, analytics, email, maps, booking systems and third-party services can change both the scope and the maintenance requirements.

Common website types

Business website

A typical business website focuses on credibility, services, contact information, locations, work examples and clear calls to action. It is often the right starting point for a company that wants a professional online presence.

E-commerce website

An online store adds product catalogs, carts, checkout, order handling, payment flows and often delivery or inventory considerations. The cost therefore depends heavily on the number of products and the required commerce features.

Custom web application

A custom application can include customer accounts, internal dashboards, workflows, databases, role-based access or other business-specific functionality. These projects should be scoped around the actual workflow rather than a fixed page count.

What about costs after launch?

The initial build is only one part of owning a website. Businesses may also need hosting, domain registration, maintenance, content updates, security checks, analytics, performance improvements and ongoing SEO work.

Some businesses handle these tasks internally. Others prefer a website partner to manage them. The important thing is to decide what support is actually needed before comparing quotes.

How should you plan a website quote?

Start with the business outcome rather than a page count. Write down what the website should help customers do, what information they need, what systems it needs to connect to and who will maintain it after launch.

A useful project brief can include:

  • Business goals and target customers
  • Required pages and key user journeys
  • Features and integrations
  • Branding and content requirements
  • Mobile and accessibility expectations
  • Launch timeline and ongoing support needs
