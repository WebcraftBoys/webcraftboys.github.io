How Much Does a Business Website Cost in 2026?

There is no single price for a business website. The right budget depends on what the website needs to do, how much content it requires, how custom the experience should be, and what needs to happen after launch.

If you're researching website development for a business, you've probably seen wildly different prices. That's because “a website” can mean anything from a small informational site to a custom platform with accounts, payments, dashboards and integrations.

This guide explains the main cost drivers so you can plan a project around what the business actually needs, rather than choosing a number without understanding the work behind it.

Webcraft's approach to pricing

Webcraft does not force every project into the same package. We prefer project-based quotes because the right scope is different for every business. Once we understand the pages, functionality, content, integrations and ongoing needs, we can define the work and provide a tailored quote.

What actually determines website cost?

The visible website is only part of the project. Cost is usually influenced by several connected decisions.

  • Number of pages: A five-page company site requires less content and production than a large site with dozens of pages.
  • Design requirements: A tailored visual system and custom layouts require more design work than adapting a simple structure.
  • Functionality: Forms, search, filtering, accounts, booking, ordering, payments and dashboards add development complexity.
  • Content: Copywriting, product information, photography, graphics and structured content all affect the workload.
  • Integrations: Connecting external services, APIs, analytics, CRMs or payment systems requires additional planning and testing.
  • Maintenance: A website that needs regular content, technical and SEO work has ongoing requirements after launch.

Think about the project in three levels

01 / Foundation

Business website

Useful for businesses that mainly need a professional online presence, clear services, contact information and strong credibility.

02 / Commerce

Online selling

Suitable when customers need to browse products or services, place orders, make payments or interact with a more structured buying journey.

03 / Custom

Web application

For projects that go beyond standard pages — for example dashboards, accounts, workflows, custom databases or business-specific tools.

These are project categories, not Webcraft's fixed price packages. A small custom requirement can change the scope, while a larger site can sometimes be straightforward when the requirements are well defined.

What should you budget for besides development?

A website project can involve more than the build itself. Before starting, consider whether you need:

  • Domain registration
  • Hosting or deployment
  • Professional copy and content preparation
  • Photography, graphics or other media
  • Analytics and conversion tracking
  • Search engine optimization
  • Ongoing maintenance and management

Not every business needs all of these as separate paid services. The useful approach is to identify which ones are actually important for the business and include them in the project plan.

How to get a realistic website quote

Before asking for a quote, prepare a short list of what the website should accomplish. You don't need technical knowledge. Even a simple brief is useful:

  • What does the business do?
  • Who needs to use the website?
  • What pages or information should be available?
  • Do visitors need to buy, book, order, register or submit information?
  • Do you already have branding and content?
  • Will someone need to maintain the website after launch?

The clearer these answers are, the easier it is to estimate the work accurately.

So, how much should you spend?

There isn't a universal number that is right for every business. A useful budget is one that matches the website's job. A simple business presence should not be priced like a custom application, and a website responsible for online orders should not be planned like a digital brochure.

If you're unsure where your project fits, start with the requirements rather than the price. Once the scope is clear, the budget becomes much easier to discuss.
