How Much Does a Business Website Cost in 2026?

There is no single price for a business website. The right budget depends on what the website needs to do, how much content it needs and how much custom work is involved.

By Webcraft·Practical guide

Website pricing can vary widely because a simple informational site and a custom web application are fundamentally different projects. Instead of choosing a price from a generic package, businesses should start with the outcome they need from the website.

What determines the cost?

1. Scope and number of pages

A focused business website may need only a handful of carefully structured pages. Larger sites may need service pages, product catalogues, landing pages, FAQs, resources or other sections. More unique content and templates generally mean more planning, design and development work.

2. Design and user experience

Design is more than choosing colours and fonts. A professional website needs a clear information hierarchy, responsive layouts, accessible interactions and calls to action that make the next step easy to understand.

3. Functionality and integrations

Forms, booking systems, online ordering, payments, dashboards, search, customer accounts and third-party integrations can add significant development requirements. The more the site needs to do, the more the project needs to be planned around those functions.

4. Content

Good copy, photography, product information and project details are part of the website experience. If content needs to be written, structured or migrated, that work should be considered in the project scope.

5. Maintenance and management

Launching a website is not always the end of the work. Businesses may need content updates, technical fixes, improvements, monitoring and ongoing management. Planning for this from the beginning can keep the website useful after launch.

6. SEO

Search visibility is influenced by technical foundations, useful content, page structure, internal linking and how well each page answers a searcher's intent. SEO is therefore better treated as an ongoing part of the website strategy rather than a single switch that guarantees rankings.

What should a business ask before choosing a website?

  • What should the website help the business achieve?
  • Who will use it and what information do they need?
  • Which features are essential at launch?
  • Who will provide and maintain the content?
  • What ongoing maintenance or SEO work will be needed?
